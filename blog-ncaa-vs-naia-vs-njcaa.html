NCAA vs NAIA vs NJCAA: which one fits you?

28 September 2026 · 6 min read

"Is D1 better?" is the question we get asked most. The honest answer: the best fit is the programme where you'll play, develop and get a degree you want — and that's not always the biggest name.

NCAA Division I

The largest universities and, on average, the highest level. Big budgets, big crowds at some schools, and very competitive rosters.

  • Scholarships: yes. Rules changed from the 2025–26 season, with many D1 schools moving from scholarship caps to roster limits. How much money is available still varies hugely between programmes.
  • Recruiting rules: strict. Coaches generally can't start recruiting conversations until 15 June after a player's US "sophomore" year (around age 16).
  • Eligibility: through the NCAA Eligibility Center: academic review of your GCSE/A-level (or equivalent) results and an amateurism check.
  • Best for: high-level players with solid grades who are ready to fight for minutes.

NCAA Division II

A strong standard of football, often with more playing time for first-years than D1. Many UK and Irish players thrive here.

  • Scholarships: yes, usually partial, shared across the squad.
  • Eligibility: NCAA Eligibility Center, with slightly different academic standards from D1.
  • Best for: good players who want a high level, real minutes and a balanced student life.

NCAA Division III

  • Scholarships: no athletic scholarships, but many D3 schools offer substantial academic and merit aid that can bring costs right down.
  • Best for: strong students who want competitive football alongside a top academic experience.

NAIA

Around 200+ smaller universities with a competitive standard. Several NAIA programmes are packed with international players.

  • Scholarships: yes, usually partial and often combined with academic aid.
  • Recruiting rules: more flexible. Coaches can talk to you earlier.
  • Eligibility: through the NAIA Eligibility Center.
  • Best for: players who want a smaller university, playing time and a faster recruiting process.

NJCAA (junior college)

Two-year colleges. Players often use junior college to improve their grades, get two seasons of US football, and then transfer to an NCAA or NAIA university.

  • Scholarships: Division I junior colleges can offer full scholarships; Division II covers costs like tuition, fees and books; Division III offers no athletic aid.
  • Best for: late developers, players whose grades need a boost, or anyone who wants a cheaper route into the US system.

How to decide

  1. Be honest about your level. Would you start, rotate or sit on the bench at this programme?
  2. Look at the course, not just the team. You'll be there four years, and the degree lasts a lifetime.
  3. Compare the real cost. Total cost per year after all athletic and academic aid.
  4. Talk to current players. Ask about the coach, playing time and how internationals settle in.
